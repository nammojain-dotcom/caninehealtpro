## 1. Physical Health Scorecards

### Body Condition Score (BCS)
*Target Ideal: 4 or 5 on a 9-point scale.*

| Score | Category | Physical Identification Signs |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1–3** | **Underweight** | Ribs, lumbar vertebrae, and pelvic bones visible from a distance. No discernible body fat. Loss of muscle mass. |
| **4–5** | **Ideal** | Ribs easily palpable with a minimal fat cover. Waist easily noted when viewed from above. Abdominal tuck visible from the side. |
| **6–7** | **Overweight** | Ribs palpable with difficulty under a thick fat layer. Waist discernible from above, but not prominent. Slight abdominal tuck. |
| **8–9** | **Obese** | Massive fat deposits over chest, spine, and base of tail. Waist and abdominal tuck absent. Fat deposits on neck and limbs. |

#### How to Perform a Physical Examination:
* **The Rib Check:** Place thumbs on the dog's spine and spread fingers over the ribcage. Ribs should feel like the knuckles on the back of your hand—individual ribs distinct under a thin layer of padding.
* **Overhead Inspection:** Stand above your pet while they are standing. A defined "hourglass" tuck behind the ribs indicates an ideal score.
* **Profile Check:** View the pet from eye level at the side. Look for a clear upward slope of the abdomen behind the ribcage (abdominal tuck).

### Baseline Vital Signs
Measure these values while your dog is at rest to establish an accurate baseline.

| Vital Metric | Healthy Reference Range | Assessment Technique |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Body Temperature** | 100.0°F – 102.5°F (37.8°C – 39.2°C) | Digital rectal thermometer with water-based lubricant. |
| **Resting Heart Rate** | 60 – 140 bpm (Smaller breeds faster) | Place palm over left chest wall directly behind the elbow. |
| **Respiration Rate** | 10 – 30 breaths/minute | Observe chest movement over a 60-second period while sleeping. |
| **Capillary Refill Time (CRT)** | < 2 seconds | Press thumb firmly on gum tissue; release and time return to pink. |
| **Mucous Membranes** | Bubblegum Pink, Moist | Lift lip and inspect gums (excluding natural pigmentation). |

## 2. Dietary Guidelines & Restrictions

### Absolute Toxicities (Never Feed)
These substances can trigger acute organ failure, system toxicity, or death:

* **Xylitol / Birch Sugar:** Found in sugar-free products, peanut butters, and dental wipes. Induces rapid insulin release leading to severe hypoglycemia and acute hepatic necrosis.
* **Grapes & Raisins:** Causes acute kidney injury (AKI). Toxic dose varies by individual; any ingestion requires intervention.
* **Allium Family (Onions, Garlic, Leeks, Chives):** Contains thiosulfates that cause oxidative damage to red blood cells, resulting in hemolytic anemia.
* **Chocolate & Methylxanthines (Caffeine/Theobromine):** Causes cardiac arrhythmias, severe agitation, seizures, and hyperthermia. Darker chocolate carries higher toxicity.
* **Macadamia Nuts:** Triggers neurological impairment, muscle weakness, tremors, hyperthermia, and vomiting.

### Condition-Specific Restrictions
* **High-Fat Meals (Bacon, Pan drippings, Skin):** Primary trigger for acute or chronic **Pancreatitis**. Breeds like Miniature Schnauzers are genetically predisposed.
* **Common Protein Allergens:** **Beef, Dairy, Chicken, and Wheat** account for the majority of cutaneous adverse food reactions (CAFR).
* **Lactose:** Adult canine gastrointestinal tracts lack sufficient lactase enzymes, causing osmotic diarrhea and flatulence.
* **Excessive Sodium:** May precipitate salt toxicosis, electrolyte imbalances, or exacerbate congestive heart failure (CHF).

### Physical Obstruction Hazards
* **Cooked Bones:** Brittle structure causes splintering upon mastication, leading to esophageal perforation, gastric tears, or severe constipation.
* **Corn Cobs:** Non-digestible fibrous cores cause mechanical gastrointestinal obstructions requiring surgical resection.
* **Stone Fruit Pits:** Choking hazards that pose obstruction risks and contain cyanogenic glycosides.

### Pillar 1: Preventative Clinical Care
1. **Examination Frequency:** 
   * Adults (1–7 yrs): Minimum 1x annual comprehensive exam.
   * Seniors (7+ yrs) & Pediatrics (<1 yr): 2x annual exams (every 6 months).
2. **Annual Diagnostic Screenings:**
   * **Heartworm / Vector-Borne Panel:** Blood test screening for *Dirofilaria immitis*, Lyme, Anaplasma, and Ehrlichia.
   * **Fecal Flotation & Antigen Testing:** Detection of intestinal parasites (Hookworms, Roundworms, Whipworms, Giardia).
   * **Senior Wellness Laboratory Panel:** Complete Blood Count (CBC), Serum Chemistry (evaluating BUN, Creatinine, ALT, ALKP), and Urinalysis.
3. **Vaccination Protocols:**
   * **Core Vaccines:** Rabies, Canine Distemper, Parvovirus, Adenovirus (DA2PP).
   * **Non-Core (Risk-Based):** *Bordetella bronchiseptica*, *Leptospira*, Lyme Disease (*Borrelia burgdorferi*), Canine Influenza.

### Pillar 2: Silent Killer Management
1. **Periodontal Disease Control:**
   * Pathogenic oral bacteria enter systemic circulation, placing recurring micro-emboli on cardiac valves, hepatic tissue, and renal nephrons.
   * **Home Care:** Tooth brushing minimum 3x weekly using veterinary enzymatic toothpaste.
   * **Clinical Care:** Professional Comprehensive Oral Health Assessment and Treatment (COHAT) under general anesthesia with dental radiographs every 12–24 months.
2. **Caloric & Weight Management:**
   * Maintaining a lean body composition increases lifespan by up to 1.8 to 2.0 years.
   * **Caloric Control:** Treats must not exceed 10% of total daily energy requirement (DER).

### Pillar 3: Environmental & Behavioral Wellness
1. **Olfactory Enrichment:** Provide structured "sniffaris" to stimulate mental processing and lower physiological stress (cortisol).
2. **Neutrality Training:** Socialization focuses on emotional neutrality in varied environments rather than forced physical interactions.
3. **Safety Infrastructure:**
   * Microchip with verified, active contact details on the national registry.
   * Crash-tested travel restraints (crates or harnesses certified by the Center for Pet Safety).

### Pillar 4: Emergency Red Flags (Immediate Medical Intervention)
Proceed immediately to an emergency facility upon noticing:
1. **Gastric Dilation-Volvulus (GDV/Bloat):** Unproductive retching, abdominal distension, tachycardia, restlessness, pale mucous membranes.
2. **Acute Respiratory Distress:** Open-mouth breathing, cyanotic (blue/purple) gums, orthopneic positioning (neck extended, elbows outward).
3. **Neurological Deficits:** Active seizures lasting > 3 minutes, cluster seizures, acute ataxia (drunk walking), loss of deep pain perception.
4. **Urethral Obstruction:** Frequent, painful, unproductive straining to urinate (common in males; life-threatening electrolyte disruption).

## 4. Emergency Protocols & First Aid Kit

### Essential First Aid Kit Supply Checklist
Store in a waterproof container and check expiration dates every 6 months:

#### Bandaging & Wound Care
* [ ] Self-Adherent Cohesive Wrap (Vet Wrap - 2" and 4")
* [ ] Non-Adherent Sterile Gauze Pads (4x4)
* [ ] Roll Gauze (Conforming bandage)
* [ ] Chlorhexidine Solution 2% (Wound lavage)
* [ ] Medical Grade Paper/Cloth Tape
* [ ] Triple Antibiotic Ointment (Hydrocortisone-free)

#### Clinical Tools
* [ ] Digital Rectal Thermometer + Water-based lubricant
* [ ] Blunt-Tip Bandage Scissors
* [ ] Fine-Point Tweezers or Tick Removal Tool
* [ ] 20mL Luer Lock Syringe (without needle for flushing wounds)
* [ ] Examination Gloves (Nitrile)
* [ ] Penlight / Small Flashlight

#### Pharmacological & Stabilization
* [ ] Diphenhydramine (Benadryl) - *Plain formula only; verify mg dosage with vet*
* [ ] Styptic Powder (Kwik Stop) or Cornstarch
* [ ] Sterile Ophthalmic Saline Flush
* [ ] Soft Mesh Muzzle or Gauze Muzzle (Use only if vomiting is not present)
* [ ] Thermal Emergency Foil Blanket

## 5. Routine Maintenance & Behavioral Standards

### Grooming & Hygiene Matrix

| Task | Frequency | Clinical Purpose |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Coat Brushing** | 2–4x Weekly | Prevents matting, distributes cutaneous lipids, allows early tumor/parasite detection. |
| **Nail Trimming** | Every 3–4 Weeks | Prevents structural gait alteration, joint strain, and quick elongation. |
| **Ear Cleaning** | Monthly / Post-swim | Maintains ear canal pH and reduces *Malassezia* or bacterial overgrowth. |
| **Bathing** | Every 1–3 Months | Cleanses skin using pH-balanced canine formulations (pH ~ 7.0–7.5). |

### Core Behavioral Skills
1. **Emergency Recall:** Daily practice using high-value rewards to ensure 100% compliance when off-leash or exposed to danger.
2. **"Leave It" Command:** Prevents ingestion of toxic items, foreign objects, or dangerous wildlife.
3. **Decompression Protocol:** Allow a 24–48 hour low-stimulation period following high-stress events (veterinary visits, travel) to return baseline cortisol levels to normal.
